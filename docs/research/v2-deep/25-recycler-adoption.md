## 2. Findings

### F1. Indore recyclers are feedstock-starved, so the core offer (more whole-equipment inflow) is genuinely attractive [S]
- UER holds ~6,000 TPA capacity (S9) but an Indore recycler reported ~450 t in FY24 (S8) — under 10% utilisation. MP as a whole processed 5,915 t (FY24) and 13,014 t (FY25) against ~58,880 TPA registered capacity (S10, `12` F1).
- Nationally, formal plants run at ~50% utilisation and informal buyers outbid them by 20–40% (S17, S18, UNVERIFIED figures).
- **Scale check (inference):** an EcoSure pilot moving 5–10 t/month would be roughly 13–27% of a ~450 t/yr recycler's actual throughput. That is material to a small Indore plant, not a rounding error. This is the strongest "yes" lever and the PRD under-sells it.

### F2. Certificate revenue is the real margin driver, and it is volatile [V floor / S market]
- Floor price per kg of equipment: ₹34 ITEW, ₹22 CEEW, ₹23 LSEEW (S11). The EC build-up itself assumes ₹25/kg for collection and transport.
- Market reality is far below: ₹6–8/kg from ghost-plant sellers (S13), a ₹5.9/kg reverse-auction bid (S12), consultants modelling ₹10–22/kg (S15, S20 context). A recycler quote puts operating cost at ₹12–16/kg (S12).
- Add metal value of roughly ₹20/kg for a mixed stream (S16, UNVERIFIED) and the recycler's revenue on EcoSure household feedstock plausibly sits in a ₹25–55/kg band, of which ₹6–34 is certificate value that depends on the Delhi HC outcome (S14, S15).
- **Implication:** recyclers will only pay hubs prices that survive a certificate-price collapse. An offtake agreement with a fixed ₹/kg price and no reopener will be refused or will be priced defensively low (which starves shops). v2's H2 has no price, no price-index, and no reopener fields at all.

### F3. The "clean chain" premium is the second "yes" lever — but v2 does not let the recycler monetise it [S / inference]
- Producers are being warned that sub-floor or unverifiable credits risk EC up to ₹93/kg (S22), and ghost-plant exposés (S13, `18` F2) make brands nervous. An honest recycler can sell certificates closer to the floor if it can show physical, citizen-origin inflow.
- v2's R7 evidence packs are **Phase 2** and producer-pull. The recycler is the party with the commercial motive to push evidence to certificate buyers, and today it can't do so in Phase 1.
- PROs (Karo Sambhav, S23) and platforms (Recykal, S24) already sell "traceability" to brands; EcoSure's differentiator is state sponsorship and citizen-origin custody. That differentiator only matters to recyclers if it helps them sell certificates.

### F4. Indore recyclers are small operations; per-lot workload and maker-checker as proposed are a real barrier [S / UNVERIFIED]
- E-Waste Samadhan lists 1–10 employees (S7); Primero Waste Solution is a 2023 company with ₹5 lakh paid-up capital (S6). UER is the largest but still a single-plant SME (S2).
- They already re-key procurement, production and sales into the CPCB portal sequentially with no edits (S21), and since 07-07-2026 must reconcile GST e-invoices for every end-product sale (S20, UNVERIFIED primary). Consultants are selling ERPs precisely because this reconciliation is done manually in Excel (S20 Infozion).
- **Estimated EcoSure workload per lot as written (inference):** confirm receipt and re-weigh (~3 min, mostly duplicated from their weighbridge), grade and decide (~3–5 min), reject photos and reason (~5 min when used), issue attestation after processing (~3 min), plus a second person for the maker-checker proposed in `18` C7 (~2 min + context switch), plus recording the portal reference later (~2 min). Roughly **10–20 minutes per lot**, higher on rejects. For a 10-lot truck that is 2–3 staff-hours — about the same as the portal work it creates value for. Acceptable only if EcoSure removes equivalent portal effort.
- Per-lot attestation "after processing" does not match how plants operate: material is pooled into shredder/dismantling batches, and CPCB itself counts production per EEE code per recycling date, not per inbound lot (`02` F3–F4). Lot-level "processed" claims force either fake precision or tracking the plant doesn't do.
- Two distinct signatories per issuance (`18` C7) is infeasible at 1–10-person firms if it is per lot; it is feasible per monthly batch.

### F5. Audit exposure is the main "no" risk — and it cuts differently for honest vs dishonest recyclers [V rules / inference]
- EcoSure creates an independent, SPCB-visible record of what physically arrived. Portal data cannot be edited (S21), mismatches take the "lower figure" (`02` F5), false info means revocation up to 3 years and non-refundable EC (S21, `02` F8), and Registered Environment Auditors now exist (S25).
- For an honest recycler, EcoSure is **audit defence**: third-party counter-weights for its procurement entries. For a recycler padding procurement, EcoSure is a liability. Reluctance to sign is itself a signal (useful to SPCB).
- The honest recycler's residual fear is **being blamed for upstream errors**: a hub weight dispute, a superseded attestation, or a rejected lot that later shows up on their record as a "mismatch" flag visible to MPPCB. v2 has no dispute-resolution window before flags go to SPCB and no distinction between "recycler fault" and "chain discrepancy".

### F6. Public verification leaks commercial information recyclers will object to [inference]
- R5 publishes issuer, date, weight and categories per attestation. If numbers are sequential or enumerable, a competitor (or a producer negotiating price) can reconstruct a recycler's monthly EcoSure throughput and category mix. Recyclers in a thin, price-sensitive certificate market (F2) will resist this.

### F7. E-invoice linkage belongs on the output side and at period level, not per lot [S, UNVERIFIED primary]
- The 07-07-2026 direction ties certificates to GST e-invoices for **end-product sales** (S20). Those invoices pool many inbound lots, so a per-lot IRN field (as floated in `18` C4) is not something a recycler can truthfully fill.
- What the recycler *can* use from EcoSure is on the **input** side: a portal-ready seller receipt per accepted transfer (name, address, GSTIN or informal, EEE code, commodity weight, date) — exactly the CPCB procurement proof (`01` G8, `02` gap 2). That directly saves portal work.

### F8. Existing inbound channels compete with EcoSure's household stream [V / S]
- All three Indore recyclers run corporate/government ITAD and bid in government auctions (S5, S7). UER advertises PAN-India collection partners (S3). Household e-waste is ~10% of formal MP volume (S8).
- EcoSure's feedstock is lower-value per kg than corporate IT unless it arrives **whole, pre-sorted by EEE code, battery-separated** (`01` 2.7). Mixed or stripped household lots will be priced down or rejected — so shop/hub quality rules (whole equipment, EEE codes) are also recycler-adoption rules.
- Government-office bulk disposal (S5, `12` F3) is a volume source recyclers already value; offering it through EcoSure (with attestation) is a sweetener.

### F9. Cash-flow mismatch in payables [inference]
- R6 has recyclers pay hubs "within agreement payment days"; hubs pay shops weekly (`06`). Recyclers realise certificate revenue only after processing, end-product sale, portal generation and producer purchase — often a quarter or more later. Expect recyclers to ask for 30–60 day terms, which squeezes hubs. v2 does not surface the trade-off or support a split (metal value now, certificate share later).

### What makes an Indore recycler say "yes" (synthesis)
1. **Guaranteed-quality feedstock**: whole equipment, EEE-coded, batteries separated, predictable monthly volume.
2. **Less portal work, not more**: EcoSure generates the CPCB procurement proof and a monthly reconciliation file; attestation is a by-product of that.
3. **A way to sell "clean" certificates**: recycler-pushed evidence packs in Phase 1, so traceability earns a price premium.
4. **Price protection**: offtake terms indexed to metal and certificate prices with a litigation reopener.
5. **Fair audit exposure**: dispute window before SPCB flags, chain vs recycler fault attribution, and no public leakage of throughput.
6. **Right-sized controls**: batch-level maker-checker and step-up auth; lot-level receipt only.
7. **Zero platform fees** (already in v2) and sponsor signalling (MPPCB/IMC preference for participating recyclers in government-office disposal).

## 3. Fit of v2 (recycler adoption lens)

| v2 element | Verdict | Why |
|------------|---------|-----|
| No commission on scrap (§1, §4) | **Strong yes-driver** | Removes the usual objection to platforms |
| Reject / partial-accept rights (R3) | **Strong yes-driver** | Protects recycler from bad lots; matches their risk |
| Offtake agreement (R2 / `06` H2) | **Gap** | No price formula, index, minimum/maximum volume, quality spec (whole equipment, EEE code, battery separation), exclusivity/ROFR, or litigation reopener |
| Per-lot attestation "after processing" (R4) | **Mismatch** | Plants process in pooled batches; creates effort and false precision |
| Capacity cap (R4, R8) | **Neutral** | EcoSure volume will rarely approach capacity (F1); low burden |
| Portal procurement proof | **Missing (biggest value gap)** | Would save recycler work and anchor the SPCB cross-check (`01`/`02`) |
| Evidence packs (R7, Phase 2) | **Too late / wrong initiator** | Recycler needs this in Phase 1 to monetise clean chain |
| Public verification (R5) | **Adoption risk** | Enumerable per-attestation weights leak throughput |
| Payables (R6) | **Gap** | No support for split terms; cash-flow tension unaddressed |
| Maker-checker / step-up (proposed by `18`) | **Risk if per lot** | Infeasible for 1–10-person firms per lot; fine per batch |
| Flags to SPCB | **Adoption risk** | No dispute window or fault attribution |

## 4. Gaps

1. No commercial terms in the offtake agreement beyond dwell, tolerance, rejects, payment days and freight.
2. No lot → batch model: lots are received individually but processing is pooled.
3. No portal-ready procurement document (seller receipt with EEE code and commodity weight).
4. No recycler-initiated evidence sharing in Phase 1.
5. No dispute window / fault attribution before discrepancy flags reach SPCB.
6. Public verification can be enumerated to reveal throughput.
7. No workload budget / time-per-lot target for the recycler UI.
8. No handling of certificate-price litigation in offtake economics (`14` does not track it; see `02` gap 10).
9. Pilot assumes one recycler signs; no evidence yet that any of UER / Samyak / Primero has been approached. Primero and Samyak are small; UER is the realistic anchor, which is also a concentration risk (`18` C12).

## 6. Score

**5.5 / 10** for the likelihood that Indore recyclers sign offtake agreements and issue attestations under v2 as written.

- **For:** recyclers are badly under-utilised (F1), v2 charges them nothing, protects them with reject and partial-accept rights, and its custody chain is useful audit defence for honest operators in a market scarred by ghost-plant scandals (F5).
- **Against:** v2 adds work without removing any (no portal procurement pack), asks for per-lot "processed" attestations that don't match plant operations (F4), leaves the offtake agreement commercially empty in a volatile certificate market (F2), delays the one feature that lets recyclers monetise clean chains (F3), and exposes them to public throughput leakage and SPCB flags without a dispute window (F5, F6).
- **With changes 1–4** the score rises to about **7.5**; with 5–9 to about **8.5**. The remaining risk is external: certificate-price litigation and whether any of the three Indore recyclers is actually willing, which only Week 0 interviews can settle.
