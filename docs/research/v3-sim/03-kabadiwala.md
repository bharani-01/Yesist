# v3 Simulation 03 — Kallu the kabadiwala: 12 weeks with EcoSure in Indore

**Date:** 2026-09-27
**PRD tested:** [`../../prd/v3-PRD.md`](../../prd/v3-PRD.md) (v3, consolidated)
**Earlier research used:** [`../v2-deep/14-informal-sector.md`](../v2-deep/14-informal-sector.md), [`../v2-deep/22-micro-shop.md`](../v2-deep/22-micro-shop.md)
**Method:** Role-play simulation of one itinerant kabadiwala (Kallu), his aggregator, and two fellow kabadiwalas over 12 weeks, run against the exact rules in the v3 PRD. Prices come from web searches done on 27 Sep 2026. **Every rupee figure is illustrative. Web prices are dealer or aggregator listings, not audited market data, and are marked UNVERIFIED.** The pilot must replace them with measured Indore prices in weeks 1–4.

---

## 1. Bottom line

- **Score: 4.5 / 10** for how well v3 brings the informal sector in, in real life.
- v3 gets the *paperwork* right: an informal collector role, any-of ID (e-Shram, NAMASTE), no GST, a written data promise, a legal agent model. These are real improvements over v2.
- v3 gets the *money* wrong for someone like Kallu. A handcart kabadiwala who follows every rule earns **about 27% less per week** and waits **9–14 days** for money he used to get the same evening. The PRD promises he keeps "the same cash business" (section 1.1 item 3, section 7.1) but nothing in the money design (sections 11.2 S5, 17) delivers same-day cash to him.
- In the simulation Kallu does not quit and does not comply. He **multi-homes**: he stays enrolled for the ID card, the society-gate access and the booked leads, sends EcoSure the low-value, heavy items (old TVs, printers), and keeps phones, cables and motors going to his aggregator, dismantled and sometimes burned. EcoSure's informal KPI goes up; the harm it was meant to stop does not go down.
- Three fixes would change this: **same-day buying at a weekly ward collection point**, **a price-parity rate card with an EPR-funded premium for the items people dismantle**, and **letting the collector book the pickup on the spot so the household incentive becomes his selling tool**.

## 2. The people

**Kallu, 45.** Pushes a handcart in the colonies east of the old city of Indore (a fictional but typical beat). Buys newspaper, iron, plastic, bottles, and whatever electronics people want gone. Lives in a rented room in a basti with his wife and three children; the eldest son (17) helps with dismantling on Sundays. Has an old Android phone with a cracked screen; reads Hindi slowly, uses WhatsApp voice notes. Has an e-Shram card his son made at a CSC in 2022, linked to the son's mobile number. Jan Dhan bank account, dormant for a year. No GST, no shop, no signboard. Pays "chai-pani" to a beat constable and sometimes to municipal staff when his cart blocks a road. Sells everything he buys to one dealer.

**Bablu Seth, the aggregator.** Runs a godown in the old-city scrap lanes. Buys all of Kallu's scrap (paper, metal, plastic and e-waste). Separates circuit boards, pays extra for phone boards, and sends bagged boards by truck to buyers linked to Seelampur (Delhi) and Moradabad. Gives kabadiwalas small cash loans (udhaar) that are repaid in scrap.

**Rekha, 38 (joins).** Waste picker turned itinerant buyer, mostly paper and plastic. Profiled under NAMASTE through a local NGO. Very little e-waste. Loses almost nothing by joining.

**Rafiq, 52 (refuses).** Bigger kabadiwala with a small rented storage room, a cycle-rickshaw and two helpers. Dismantles fans, mixers, TVs and phones for copper and boards. E-waste is about half his profit.

## 4. Kallu's week before EcoSure (the baseline)

Assumed typical week of e-waste (UNVERIFIED; about 30 kg alongside roughly 250–300 kg of paper, iron and plastic):

| Item | Buys from households | Sells to Bablu intact | Sells to Bablu after dismantling / burning |
|------|---------------------:|----------------------:|-------------------------------------------:|
| 3 dead phones | ₹120 | ₹330 (₹110 each) | ₹420 (board, battery, rest) |
| 4 kg cables | ₹160 | ₹600 (insulated, ~₹150/kg to him) | ₹936 (≈1.8 kg burnt copper at ~₹520) |
| 12 kg small appliances (mixer, iron, fan, router, set-top box, chargers) | ₹144 | ₹360 (₹30/kg) | ₹750 (motor copper, boards, aluminium, iron, plastic) |
| 1 TV / monitor | ₹80 | ₹200 | ₹300 (board plus plastic and metal; glass dumped) |
| ¼ laptop (one a month) | ₹62 | ₹125 | ₹125 (sold whole) |
| **Total** | **₹566** | **₹1,615** | **₹2,531** |

- E-waste margin if sold intact: **₹1,049/week**.
- E-waste margin with dismantling and burning (what he actually does): **₹1,965/week**. Dismantling roughly doubles his e-waste margin for about one family day of work.
- Other scrap margin: about ₹2,200/week.
- Harassment and bribes: about −₹150/week.
- **Normal week: about ₹4,000 net. Diwali weeks: about ₹6,000** (households clear out).

He is paid in cash the same evening, every day. His working capital is about ₹1,500–2,000, turned over daily.

## 5. The 12 weeks

The pilot starts in early October, during the Swachhata Hi Seva window the PRD recommends (section 23). This puts Diwali (8 November 2026) in weeks 5–6.

### Week 1 — Hearing about it
Rekha tells him at the chai stall that "sarkar ka naya card" lets kabadiwalas enter societies. Then the ward sanitary inspector announces an EcoSure camp at the ward office. Kallu's first thought is not money but danger: *will they write down what I do and send it to the police?* Bablu, when asked, says: "They will take your Aadhaar, then GST will come, then they will close you down. Remember what happened to the rag pickers here." (The PRD itself cites Indore's 2018 eviction history in the research; section 3.7 and the informal research F5.)

### Week 2 — Onboarding (PRD section 11.2 S1, section 8.5)
- The camp accepts his e-Shram card. Good: v3's any-of ID works. But the OTP goes to his **son's** phone, which is linked to the card; his son is at work. The camp staff wait. Half a day lost.
- The payout account must be his. His Jan Dhan account is dormant; he goes to the bank the next day to re-do KYC. Another half day.
- The **agent agreement** is read to him in Hindi by the operator's field staff. He understands "intact items only" and "the recycler's rates". He does not understand "chargeback", "exclusivity", "storage deadline" or "72-hour dispute window". He signs because Rekha signed.
- The data notice ("shared with enforcement only through the lawful request process") is read out. He does not know what a lawful request is. What reassures him is the ID card with his photo and the MPPCB logo.
- Provisional approval arrives by SMS in three working days. The SMS is in Hindi but uses words he cannot read quickly; Rekha reads it for him.

**Cost:** one working day, about ₹450. Rekha, whose NGO pre-filled her forms, loses an hour.

### Week 3 — First rate card and first bookings (sections 11.2 S2, S3, S6; 12.2 R3)
The rate card arrives as a WhatsApp image. Kallu has his son compare it with Bablu's prices:

| Item | EcoSure recycler rate (simulated) | Bablu, intact | Bablu, what Kallu really earns after dismantling |
|------|---------------------------------:|--------------:|------------------------------------------------:|
| Dead phone | ₹80 each | ₹110 | ₹140 |
| Cables | ₹120/kg | ₹150/kg | ₹234/kg |
| Small appliances | ₹25/kg | ₹30/kg | ₹62/kg |
| TV / monitor | ₹15/kg (≈₹120 each) | ₹200 each | ₹300 each |
| Laptop | ₹350 | ₹500 | ₹500 |
| Agent handling margin | ₹3/kg (the PRD mentions a margin in section 17.3 but never sets it) | — | — |

The PRD's own worked example (section 17.3) uses ₹60 per phone, which is lower still. **On every line the formal rate is below what the aggregator pays for the same intact item**, before counting the dismantling uplift. The recycler rate card has no floor and no link to the informal price (section 12.2 R3 says only "reviewed weekly").

He gets three booked pickups in his beat. What happens at the doors:

1. **The price flip.** In a booked pickup he must pay the household *the recycler's published price* (section 10.2 C7), then gets the same amount back plus the handling margin. His normal buy-low/sell-high spread disappears. On a booked pickup he earns ₹3/kg, about ₹20.
2. **Pay first, code second.** Section 10.2 C6 says the household gives the 4-digit code "only after weighing and payment". At one flat, the aunty's code SMS never arrived. Kallu has already paid ₹140 from his pocket. He tries the operator; the call-back rule for missed calls is "within 4 working hours" (section 10.2 C1). He leaves. The pickup is stuck without `handed_over`, so he fears he will not be reimbursed (section 16.2 gate).
3. **IMEI scanning.** Of three phones, one is a sealed modern phone that will not power on, so `*#06#` is impossible; the IMEI is printed microscopically on the SIM tray and the camera cannot read it. One is an old feature phone with the label under a missing battery. Only one scans. The PRD accepts "category and weight" as a fallback (section 9.3) but the pilot gate wants ≥ 40% of phones scanned by week 8 (section 25.3) and ≥ 60% linked passports later. **Dead phones are exactly the phones kabadiwalas get**, and the PRD never discusses them.
4. **Battery check.** A mixer and an iron: "no battery". A power bank with a slightly puffed cell: he must refuse it (section 10.2 C6). The household gives it to him anyway "for free". He sells it to Bablu. Nothing in EcoSure catches this.

He also decides to test "intact only" honestly: this week his own door-to-door small appliances, TV and phones go into the EcoSure sack instead of being dismantled.

### Week 4 — Sealing and delivering the lot (sections 11.2 S4, 16.4, 15.1)
- He has no storage. The sealed sack sits in his room with his children.
- The pilot has **no hub**; agents "deliver to the recycler's gate" (section 16.1). The recycler's plant is in an industrial area roughly 20 km away (location simulated; verify). A handcart cannot go. He hires a loading tempo with Rekha: ₹400, split two ways.
- The gate weighs his lot on a calibrated electronic scale: 6% lower than his hanging spring balance. Outside the 5% tolerance, so a dispute opens and "the lower reading is settled on time" (section 16.4 step 5). He loses about ₹50 and a morning.
- The gate staff are polite but busy. There is no chair, no water, and his lot is processed after a truck from a bulk consumer.

### Weeks 5–6 — Diwali: the float breaks (sections 11.2 S5, 17.4)
- Households clear out before Diwali. His EcoSure queue shows 12 bookings a week. Each one means paying the household the rate-card price upfront.
- His money from the week 3–4 lot has not arrived: reimbursement is "within 7 days of recycler receipt" (section 11.2 S5), and receipt was only days ago. The **advance** for a new agent is 20% of average weekly accepted value (section 17.4). His average is about ₹700, so the advance is **₹140**.
- He accepts half the bookings. At the others, he knocks as a normal kabadiwala and buys for cash outside the system. In EcoSure these show up as accepted-then-cancelled. This is the leakage pattern the micro-shop research warned about (22-micro-shop F6), and v3 has no flag for it (section 18.2 has no "accept then cancel" control).
- Bablu sees Kallu is short and offers ₹3,000 udhaar, "repay in scrap, no hurry". Kallu takes it. He is now tied to Bablu for the next two months.
- **Week 6:** the first settlement lands, day 9 after receipt: ₹900 by bank transfer, with an English SMS ("Settlement paid"). He does not know which pickups it covers.

### Week 7 — The chargeback (sections 17.4, 11.2 S5, 11.2 S8)
The recycler's unit scan (section 12.2 R4) finds two problems in his lot:
- A phone with no battery. The household had removed it. The recycler marks it as "tampered / possible dismantling".
- A feature phone he logged as "phone" was graded as "small appliance".

Result: a **chargeback of ₹240** against his next settlement, and the household's incentive for that phone is reversed. The aunty calls him angry: "You said government would pay me ₹50." The chargeback screen shows "evidence" — a photo and a text reason in Hindi. He cannot read it. The 72-hour dispute window (section 11.2 S8) counts from recycler receipt, and it has passed. There is no voice explanation and no field visit.

This is the single worst moment of the 12 weeks for his trust. The amount is small; the feeling is "the system can take money from me and I cannot argue".

### Week 8 — The police stop
A constable stops his cart with the sealed sack. Kallu shows the EcoSure ID card. The constable has never heard of it. Kallu pays ₹100 anyway. The PRD's protection promise is about **data** (section 16.6 step 4, section 21.3), but his real daily fear is the **street**. The legal cover comes from an MPPCB direction (section 5.2), which police and municipal enforcement staff will not have seen.

Two days later the ward sanitary inspector recognises the card and lets him into a gated society for an IMC ward drive. He buys 40 kg of newspaper there too. **This is the first week where joining clearly pays.**

### Weeks 9–12 — Settling into multi-homing
By week 9 Kallu has worked out his own rules:
- **Booked EcoSure pickups:** yes, when he has cash. They bring him into houses and societies he could not enter, and the same households sell him paper and iron.
- **Heavy low-value items** (CRT TVs, old printers, broken plastic appliances that Bablu pays almost nothing for): into the EcoSure sack. The formal rate beats "nothing".
- **Phones, cables, fans, mixers from his own door-to-door buying:** to Bablu, dismantled. His son still burns cable insulation on Sunday behind the basti.
- He shares a tempo with Rekha every two weeks to the recycler gate.

The recycler's dashboard starts to show the **"high-value share of agent lots falling"** tripwire (section 18.3). The recycler's ops manager, Arjun, suspects Kallu is cherry-picking but has no proof and no tool to help him. The operator's response ladder is unclear; the PRD lists the tripwire but no playbook for a single agent.

**End of week 12:** Kallu stays enrolled. He would quit if there were a second chargeback, if the society access stopped, or if Bablu made him choose.

## 6. Rupee math per week: joining vs not joining

"Joining" follows Kallu's actual behaviour (tries full compliance in weeks 3–4, breaks during Diwali, multi-homes from week 7). "Cash in hand" is what he could spend that week. All figures ₹, rounded, UNVERIFIED.

| 2 | Onboarding camp, bank KYC | 4,000 | 3,550 | 3,550 | 0 |
| 3 | First bookings; own items sent intact | 4,000 | 3,000 | 1,900 | 900 |
| 4 | Seals lot; tempo; weight dispute | 4,000 | 2,800 | 1,300 | 1,750 |

| 6 | Diwali; first settlement ₹900 | 6,050 | 4,900 | 5,600 | 1,100 |
| 7 | Chargeback −₹240; starts multi-homing | 4,000 | 3,700 | 4,300 | 400 |
| 8 | Police stop (−₹100); first society drive | 4,000 | 4,150 | 3,900 | 500 |
| 9 | Multi-homing; more leads | 4,000 | 4,400 | 4,300 | 500 |
| 10 | Multi-homing; second society | 4,000 | 4,500 | 4,400 | 550 |
| 11 | Multi-homing | 4,000 | 4,600 | 4,550 | 550 |
| 12 | Multi-homing | 4,000 | 4,600 | 4,550 | 550 |
| **Total** | | **52,100** | **48,800** | | |

**Reading the table:**
- Over 12 weeks, joining costs him about **₹3,300 (−6%)**, almost all in weeks 2–7: onboarding time, the intact-only test, the missed Diwali trade, the chargeback.
- From week 8 he is **₹400–600 a week better off**, but only because he multi-homes. The gain comes from **access** (societies, ward drives, booked leads) and from dumping low-value items onto EcoSure, not from the rate card.
- In weeks 3–4 his cash in hand drops to **₹1,300–1,900**, below what his family needs (roughly ₹2,500 a week, UNVERIFIED). This is the moment most kabadiwalas would quit. Kallu survived it with an aggregator loan, which ties him tighter to the informal chain.

**Counterfactual: full compliance all 12 weeks** (every e-waste item intact through EcoSure, no dismantling, no burning):

| Line (normal week) | ₹ |
|--------------------|--:|
| E-waste: rate card on 30 kg incl. ₹3/kg handling − purchase cost (₹1,317 − ₹566) | 751 |
| Booked pickups: handling margin plus side-scrap from the same houses | 186 |
| Other scrap | 2,200 |
| Tempo share to recycler gate | −200 |
| Bribes (slightly fewer with ID card) | −100 |
| **Net** | **≈ 2,840 (−29% vs ₹4,000)** |
| Cash in hand in a typical week (e-waste money arrives 9–14 days later) | ≈ 1,200 |

Over 12 weeks full compliance earns about **₹38,000 vs ₹52,100 (−27%)**. No rational kabadiwala does this.

**With the three highest-leverage fixes** (section 9): same-day buying at a ward point, price parity through an EPR-funded premium (about ₹8–40 per item or kg on the lines people dismantle, plus ₹20 per phone collector bounty), and collector spot-booking that lets him offer the household the incentive:

| Line (normal week) | ₹ |
|--------------------|--:|
| E-waste at premium rate card, same-day cash (≈ ₹1,673 − ₹566) | 1,107 |
| Extra e-waste he wins from other kabadiwalas because he can offer the household the government incentive | 220 |
| Booked pickups, side-scrap, society access | 486 |
| Other scrap | 2,200 |
| Tempo (no longer needed; ward point) | 0 |
| Bribes | −50 |
| **Net** | **≈ 3,960 (parity), all same-day** |

At parity, the non-money benefits (ID card, PM-JAY through NAMASTE, no burning, no lead exposure for his son) tip the decision. The premium costs about ₹300 per collector per week; for 100 collectors that is about **₹15–16 lakh a year**, small against the ₹6.2 crore budget (section 26) and well inside the ₹22/kg EPR certificate floor value that recyclers earn on the same material (floor under litigation; UNVERIFIED that it survives).

## 7. The aggregator's reaction (Bablu)

| Week | What Bablu does | Why |
|-----:|-----------------|-----|
| 1–2 | Spreads fear: "Aadhaar → GST → police" | Protect supply; costs him nothing |
| 3–4 | Raises phone and board prices for kabadiwalas who joined by ₹10–20 per phone | Boards are his margin; he can afford to outbid a recycler selling to the formal chain |
| 5 | Offers udhaar (₹3,000) to Kallu and others short of cash during Diwali | Ties them to him in scrap repayment; the classic informal credit lock |
| 7–8 | Hints that if Kallu "gives the good things to the government", Bablu will pay less for his paper and iron | **Bundling**: Bablu buys *all* of Kallu's scrap. E-waste is 30 kg; the rest is 250+ kg. Kallu cannot risk that relationship |
| 10–12 | Asks the operator whether *he* can become an EcoSure agent shop | If he enrols, he gets leads, an ID, and a legal receipt for the intact items he chooses to pass on, while still dismantling the rest |

The PRD has **no aggregator in its stakeholder map** (section 7.1). It treats the informal sector as individual collectors who can be recruited one by one. In reality the aggregator controls credit, bundles all scrap types, and can outbid the recycler on boards because he does not pay for pollution control. Any design that ignores him will be undercut by him.

## 8. Two other kabadiwalas

### Rekha — joins and stays
- Paper and plastic are 95% of her trade; e-waste is 3–5 kg a week, mostly chargers and a broken phone. Intact-only costs her perhaps ₹60 a week.
- The NGO that profiled her under NAMASTE handles her onboarding. She already has a photo ID card from the ULB.
- What she gains: **entry to gated societies**. Under the Solid Waste Management Rules 2026, bulk generators must hand recyclables to *authorised* waste pickers (informal research F2). An EcoSure agent card plus her NAMASTE card makes her "authorised" in the eyes of society guards. Her paper intake rises about 30%.
- Her problems: the family phone is her husband's; she wants payouts to her own account (the PRD allows "payout account" but does not address shared phones or women's control of money).
- **Verdict:** EcoSure works for Rekha, but mostly as a **recognition and access** scheme, not an e-waste scheme.

### Rafiq — refuses
- E-waste is about half his profit (roughly ₹3,500 of ₹7,000 a week, UNVERIFIED). Intact-only would cost him ₹1,800–2,200 a week.
- He has a storage room. A "dated, weighed, geo-tagged" record of his trade is exactly what he fears (micro-shop research F7). He has paid bribes to keep that room open.
- He owes Bablu ₹15,000.
- By week 10, two societies in his area start telling guards "only card-holders". Rafiq loses about 15% of his paper supply. He complains to the local corporator. This is the **grievance tripwire** in section 18.3 ("any organised statement" is amber). The PRD monitors this but offers Rafiq nothing: no dismantling-transition path, no route for a small storage room to become a lawful sorting point, no training into repair or refurbishment work.
- **Verdict:** EcoSure makes Rafiq's life slightly harder and gives him no reason to change. Multiply him by the hundreds of small dismantlers in any city and this becomes the political story (pre-mortem section 27.1; informal research F5).

## 10. PRD gaps (with v3 sections)

| # | Gap | Where in v3 | Why it matters in real life |
|---|-----|-------------|-----------------------------|
| G1 | **The "same cash business" promise has no mechanism for itinerant collectors.** Section 16.6 says they "sell to agents or recycler gates at the published rate" but never says when they are paid. The only agent money rule is reimbursement within 7 days of recycler receipt | 1.1 item 3; 7.1; 11.2 S5; 16.6 step 3; 17.2 Rail A | A kabadiwala's capital turns over daily. A 9–14 day cycle stops his buying |
| G2 | **No compensation for giving up dismantling.** Intact-only is right legally, but the rate card has no floor, no link to informal prices, and no EPR premium for the items people dismantle (cables, motors, phones, boards) | 5.2; 11.3; 12.2 R3; 17.3 (₹60 phone example) | This is the core reason Kallu multi-homes. The v2 micro-shop research (22, rec. 2) asked for this and v3 did not add it |
| G3 | **In booked pickups the collector's margin collapses to an undefined "handling margin".** He must pay the household the recycler's price and gets the same back | 10.2 C7; 17.3 step 5 | Booked pickups earn him ~₹20. Leads are the only value, and leads leak |
| G4 | **Unbooked door-to-door buying does not fit the model.** A pickup needs a requester, a code and a booking; Kallu's trade is knocking on doors. Assisted booking exists only "at shops and ward offices" | 10.2 C1; 16.2; 16.6 | Most of his volume can never be recorded, and the household incentive, EcoSure's strongest tool, is unavailable to him |
| G5 | **Pay first, code second puts the risk on the poorest party.** If the SMS fails, he has paid and cannot close the pickup | 10.2 C6; 16.2 `handed_over` gate; 16.3 steps 7–8 | Every code failure is his money at risk |
| G6 | **Dead phones cannot give an IMEI.** `*#06#` needs a working phone; labels are missing or microscopic | 9.3; 9.5 PP3; 11.2 S3; 25.3 week-8 gate (≥ 40% scanned) | Kabadiwalas mostly get dead phones; the gate may fail for reasons no training fixes |
| G7 | **Chargebacks have no protection for micro agents**: no cap, no voice explanation, dispute window counted from recycler receipt, not from the chargeback notice, and incentive reversals hit the collector's standing with households | 11.2 S5, S8; 17.4 | One unexplained ₹240 deduction destroyed more trust than any rate difference |
| G8 | **No collection logistics for handcart collectors in the pilot.** No hub, and "deliver to the recycler's gate" | 11.2 S4; 15.1; 16.1; 16.4 | A tempo costs ₹400 a trip; for 30 kg a week that is ₹6–13/kg, bigger than the handling margin |

## 11. Fixes

### Highest leverage (do these first)

1. **Same-day buying at weekly ward collection points (fixes G1, G8, G9, G5 in part).** The recycler, as principal, runs a buying van or desk at each ward office or IMC point once or twice a week (Karo Sambhav precedent). It weighs, grades, seals and **pays the collector on the spot from Rail A escrow**. No lot-building at home, no tempo, no 7-day wait. Record it as a `Transfer` from the collector to the recycler with a receiver weigh record. Target: collector paid the same day for ≥ 90% of accepted weight. Add a pilot gate for "collector cash-cycle time".

2. **Price-parity rate card with an EPR-funded premium (fixes G2, G3, G15).** Every category line shows base rate + producer/EPR premium, with higher premiums for items people dismantle (cables, motor appliances, phones, laptops, board-rich devices). The operator publishes a weekly Indore informal price index; the target is **formal intact price ≥ aggregator intact price + about half of the dismantling uplift**. Add a **collector per-device bounty** for phones and laptops (for example ₹20), funded from producer take-back escrow (Rail B′), paid same day or T+1. Set the "agent handling margin" explicitly (for example ₹3–5/kg). Measure high-value share of informal lots, not just tonnes.

3. **Collector spot-booking (fixes G4, G5).** Let an enrolled informal collector create a pickup at the door with the household's phone number. The household gets the SMS code and the scheme incentive; the collector enters the code **before** paying (code after weighing, pay on code). Controls: per-collector daily cap, household-phone and address dedupe (already in section 17.4), random operator call-backs to households. This turns the government incentive into **Kallu's selling tool** against non-enrolled buyers, without the state paying him directly.

### Next most important

4. **Chargeback protections for micro agents (G7):** cap any single deduction at 20% of that settlement; explain every chargeback by voice note; dispute window of 7 days from the notice; operator field visit before any chargeback over ₹200; never reverse a household incentive for a grading difference (only for proven fraud).
5. **Dead-phone rule (G6):** accept "no readable ID" without penalty; allow a photo of the SIM-tray or label; count the week-8 scan gate only against phones that power on or have a readable label.
6. **Street protection (G11):** a joint circular from the Police Commissionerate and IMC recognising the EcoSure agent card; a QR on the card that any officer can scan to see "valid agent of [recycler], valid until [date]" and nothing else.
7. **Aggregator transition path (G10, G17):** add aggregators and small dismantlers as a stakeholder. Offer them enrolment as agent shops under stricter rules (no dismantling on site, weekly recycler audit) or a route into recycler jobs or refurbishment. Offer collectors a small interest-free onboarding float (for example ₹2,000 from recycler escrow, recovered over 8 weeks) to break the udhaar lock.
8. **Voice-first agent channel (G12):** Hindi voice notes for rate changes, settlements (which pickups were paid) and chargebacks; an agent IVR line; the rate card as a picture list with item photos.
9. **Scope exclusivity per pickup (G13):** state in the agreement that only items recorded in EcoSure are exclusive to the recycler; his other trade is his own.
10. **Leakage and cherry-picking playbook (G14):** add accept-then-cancel and declared-vs-collected device count flags; a response ladder of conversation, then fewer leads, then suspension with appeal, never public listing.
11. **Safety and children (G16):** Hindi declaration of no children handling e-waste; PPE through NAMASTE; a "no burning" module with the price math showing mechanical stripping or selling insulated wire to the recycler at granulation value.

## 12. Score

**4.5 / 10** for how well v3 integrates the informal sector in real life.

- **What v3 does well (why it is not lower):** a named `informal_collector` role with no shop needed; e-Shram and NAMASTE accepted; no GST; a written data promise; a legal agent model that genuinely gives kabadiwalas a lawful place; a grievance tripwire for non-enrolled kabadiwalas; a before-and-after informal survey. Rekha's story shows the recognition and access benefits are real.
- **What pulls it down:** the money design was written for shops and citizens, not handcart collectors. The collector is never paid same day, is never compensated for giving up dismantling, earns almost nothing on booked pickups, carries the risk of code failures and chargebacks, and must haul sacks 20 km. The aggregator, who actually controls the kabadiwala's choices through credit and bundled buying, is absent from the design.
- **Likely real-world outcome as written:** enrolment looks healthy, informal-sourced tonnes rise, but they are mostly heavy low-value items. Phones, boards and copper keep flowing to aggregators and to burning. The informal KPI is met while the health and pollution problem the problem statement cares about is barely touched.
- **With fixes 1–3:** about 7 / 10. With 1–11: about 7.5–8 / 10, still subject to real Indore prices from the pilot.
