# v3 Simulation 06 — IMC politics: will Indore Municipal Corporation actually run EcoSure?

**Simulation:** Real-life decision chain at Indore Municipal Corporation (IMC) for the EcoSure v3 pilot  
**PRD tested:** `docs/prd/v3-PRD.md` (v3, 2026-09-27), read in full  
**Prior research used:** `docs/research/v2-deep/12-madhya-pradesh.md`, `31-political-stakeholders.md`, `36-partnerships.md`  
**Date:** 2026-09-27  
**Method:** Web search on IMC e-waste collection, its vendors, the Swachh Survekshan 2025-26 toolkit, IMC staffing and labour disputes, and commissioner tenure; then a role-played steering committee and five follow-up negotiations. The people in the simulation are **composite characters based on posts, not real individuals**. Their lines are an analyst's judgement of how each post would behave, not quotes.  
**Convention:** Anything from a single news report, an aggregator, or search synthesis is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Simulation assumptions are marked **ASSUMPTION**.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**Score: 5 / 10** for how well v3 works with IMC in real life.

v3 made the right strategic call: it stopped building a rival network and put IMC in as co-sponsor, layering EcoSure over the city's existing e-waste flow (sections 5.1, 5.3). That is the difference between a programme IMC tolerates and one it kills. But the PRD handles the city in **one line** (G7, section 14.3) and one persona (Rakesh, section 7.2). Everything the simulation found hard is missing:

1. **The city flow does not fit the citizen pickup model.** Garbage-vehicle drivers cannot run handover codes, wipe checks, battery checks and IMEI scans at 800 vehicles' worth of doorsteps. The PRD has no separate lot-level "municipal stream" workflow.
2. **Honest weighing could make IMC's own numbers shrink.** IMC's public claim is 2–2.5 tonnes a day. If the verified figure at authorised recycler gates is much lower, EcoSure's first headline is "cleanest city overstated e-waste", and IMC will stop the programme before that happens.
3. **The existing vendors lose the most and are not at the table.** The PRD never says what happens to their contract, their fee, or their high-value pickings.
4. **The PRD overstates the Swachh Survekshan payoff.** The 2025-26 toolkit has no e-waste tonnage indicator; the real payoff is evidence for the RRR-centre and forward-linkage indicators.
5. **Commissioner churn is not planned for.** Indore has had three commissioners in about five months. The pilot runs 52–66 weeks.

With the fixes in section 8 (mostly governance, a municipal-stream workflow, a baseline-reconciliation phase, and a vendor transition clause), this would rise to about **7 / 10**. The remaining risk is political and can only be tested in a real first meeting.

## 4. Meeting simulation

### 4.1 Steering committee, meeting 1 (IMC HQ, month 0 of Stage −1)

Present: the members listed in section 5.1 (Environment, UDHD, MPPCB, IMC, MPSEDC, Finance). The Additional Commissioner (SWM) attends for IMC. **Neither vendor, the MIC member, nor any union is on the committee** (section 5.1 lists departments only).

> **Environment (chair):** The joint order is signed. EcoSure layers over IMC's collection. We need the IMC MoU and the 12-month baseline by channel within 8 weeks (section 25.2).
>
> **Additional Commissioner, SWM:** Before we sign anything: under the SWM Rules 2026 we are already building our own portal, with certificate-linked tracking of every MRF and concessionaire. Why should my zones enter the same truckload twice?
>
> **MPSEDC:** EcoSure can take a feed from your portal.
>
> **Additional Commissioner:** Then write that down. The PRD says "IMC logs vehicle and ward-point collections" (G7). Logs *where*? On whose phone? I have 800 vehicles.
>
> **MPPCB:** Legally, the PRD says every drop point is an agent of a registered recycler (section 5.2). Does that include IMC?
>
> **UDHD:** A municipal corporation will not be the "agent" of a private company. Find another legal form.
>
> **Finance:** Who pays the ₹20/kg IMC pays vendors today? If EcoSure moves material value to recycler escrow (section 17.2), is IMC's fee still needed?
>
> **Additional Commissioner:** That fee is in a contract. I will not reopen it mid-year.
>
> **Commissioner (joins late):** Two things. First, nothing goes public about Indore's e-waste numbers without IMC signing off. Second, what do we get in Swachh Survekshan?
>
> **Environment:** "Ward tonnes for Swachh Survekshan" (G7).
>
> **Commissioner:** Survekshan does not score e-waste tonnes. It scores RRR centres, segregation, forward linkages, and citizen feedback. Give me documents the assessors accept.

**Outcome:** The meeting agrees in principle but signs nothing. Actions: IMC to name a nodal officer; the programme to answer the "second portal", legal form, fee and publication questions within 4 weeks. **Stage −1 slips by 4–6 weeks on the first day.**

### 4.2 Negotiation A: logging IMC vehicle collections

*Additional Commissioner (SWM), a zonal officer, a sanitary inspector, the operator, and vendor A.*

- The operator proposes that drivers scan IMEIs and record items at the doorstep, as the capability matrix implies (section 8.3: city has "Legacy registration at collection — W" and "Accept, collect, weigh — F").
- **Sanitary inspector:** "The driver has two minutes a house and a wet-waste bin in his other hand. He isn't going to type anything."
- **Union (by letter):** No new duty for drivers without an order, training time and allowance. No use of app data for attendance or penalties.
- **Workable compromise:** The citizen does nothing new. E-waste stays in the vehicle's existing compartment. **The first custody event happens at the zonal transfer station or e-waste bay**, where a vendor or recycler clerk (not IMC staff) weighs each vehicle's e-waste on a calibrated scale, seals it into a lot, and tags the vehicle number and ward. The vehicle's existing GPS trail from the SWM control room becomes the route evidence, not new AIS-140 devices (section 20.5). IMEI scanning happens **at the recycler gate**, never on the vehicle.
- **Consequence the PRD does not show:** For the city stream there is no handover code, no citizen incentive, and no per-household record. Two v3 KPIs therefore barely apply to this, the largest flow: device traceability ≥60% at collection, and consumer participation ≥5% (section 24.1).

### 4.3 Negotiation B: data ownership

- **Commissioner:** "The data is collected by IMC vehicles in IMC wards. It is IMC data."
- **Environment:** Section 5.4 says "the department owns the platform and data", and section 21.3 names "the sponsoring department" as data fiduciary. With a joint order there are two departments. IMC is a separate statutory body and a third party.
- **MIC member:** "No ward-level numbers go on data.gov.in (G9) until the council has seen them. A corporator will be attacked if their ward shows zero."
- **Agreed position (simulated):** IMC is the owner and joint data fiduciary for city-stream records and for citizen data from IMC drives. The state holds a licence to use aggregates. Ward-level public release requires 30 days' notice to IMC, and small cells are suppressed as the PRD already says. **IMC can read raw exports of its own stream at any time**, and that right must survive a change of software vendor.
- **Unresolved:** Who answers an RTI request for ward data: IMC's PIO or the department's PIO (G10, SP-12)?

### 4.4 Negotiation C: Swachh Survekshan credit

- The Commissioner's aide reads out the 2025-26 toolkit. There is no e-waste tonnage line. The indicators EcoSure can help with are **RRR centre functionality and forward linkage (3.9, 150 marks), dry waste forward linkages (3.4), segregation and collection (section 2), bulk waste generators (3.7), and citizen feedback on RRR awareness** (F8).
- **What IMC actually wants from EcoSure:** (1) a recycler-signed forward-linkage record for every e-waste lot leaving RRR centres, MRF bays and drop boxes, in the format of the state "RRR Centre MoU with disposal logbook clause"; (2) photos and geotags an assessor can check; (3) a citizen-facing "your e-waste went to X recycler" message to lift feedback scores; (4) bulk waste generator e-waste disposal receipts for the new EBWGR cell (F3).
- **Honest note:** Because Indore is already in the Super Swachh League (F9), a few extra marks matter less than **avoiding embarrassment**. The reputational downside of EcoSure (numbers shrinking) outweighs the marks upside, and the Commissioner knows it.

### 4.5 Negotiation D: the vendors, the baseline, and "additional tonnes"

*Additional Commissioner, vendors A and B, recycler representatives, and the operator. The Commissioner is not present.*

- **Vendor A (privately to the Additional Commissioner):** "If every sack is weighed at a registered recycler's gate, and your device-count leakage check (section 18.3) compares my counts with the recycler's, you'll see I sell the phones and boards separately. That's how the ₹20/kg rate works. Take that away and I need ₹35/kg." (**ASSUMPTION**, but cherry-picking is the leakage pattern the PRD itself names in section 18.2.)
- **Vendor A's legal problem:** Under the agent model (section 5.2), vendor A can only carry on as an agent of a registered recycler, which likely means vendor B, its competitor. Vendor A sees EcoSure as a device to hand its contract to vendor B.
- **Vendor B:** "Make us the principal for the whole city stream." Other recyclers (Primero, Samyak) then complain to MPPCB about favouritism. The PRD requires only "at least 1 recycler" (section 25.2) and says nothing about neutrality between recyclers.
- **Baseline fight:** Section 24.1 counts only tonnes above a 12-month baseline "agreed with sponsors", by channel. IMC's baseline would be its claimed 2–2.5 t/day (60–75 t/month, section 26.2). If weighing at recycler gates shows, say, 20–30 t/month of true Schedule I e-waste (**ASSUMPTION**; the size of the gap is unknown, F2), then:
  - "additional tonnes" is **negative** for months, and the amber and red tripwires fire (section 18.3);
  - the pilot's 8 t/month gate (section 25.3) is met trivially by relabelling, which is exactly what the additionality rule is meant to stop;
  - the public headline becomes "Indore's e-waste figure was 2–3 times inflated".
- **What the Additional Commissioner demands:** No comparison between IMC's historic claim and verified tonnes appears in any public or state report. The baseline is **re-measured**, not taken from past claims.
- **Simulated resolution:** The first 6 weeks of the manual pilot become a **"verified baseline" phase**: weigh everything and publish nothing. The verified figure becomes the baseline, and "additional tonnes" counts from there. Historic claims are archived and never charted. The vendor contract runs unchanged to its end date; the **next** empanelment requires EcoSure recording, a named registered recycler, and open recycler participation. Vendor A is offered a documented agent role under any registered recycler of its choice, not only vendor B.

### 4.6 Negotiation E: staff workload and the driver union

- **Sanitary inspector:** A realistic time-and-motion estimate: if SIs must confirm every ward drive and every vehicle log, that is 20–40 minutes a day per ward (**ASSUMPTION**), on top of SS inspections and water-crisis follow-up. The PRD persona ("Rakesh wants to count e-waste") is the programme's wish, not the SI's.
- **Union:** Accepts no new driver tasks. Would accept a **zone-level** recognition such as "cleanest e-waste zone", as long as it is not an individual incentive, because individual incentives reopen the wage-parity fight they just won in court (F6). Any use of EcoSure data to discipline drivers for "missing" e-waste will be treated as surveillance.
- **MIC member:** Backs the union. The opposition is already filming sanitation workers' wage complaints (F6). "No EcoSure announcement while sanitation workers are protesting."
- **Resolution:** All city-stream logging is done by vendor or recycler clerks at transfer stations, funded from the operator budget or material value, not by IMC staff. SIs get a **read-only weekly ward summary** and one approval task per ward drive. Drivers get training only on keeping swollen batteries out of the e-waste compartment (a fire risk the PRD takes seriously in S3), and that is framed as safety, not new work.

### 4.7 Month 7: the Commissioner is transferred

**Scenario (ASSUMPTION, but base-rate likely; F7):** Seven months into the programme, a new Commissioner arrives after a state reshuffle. The Additional Commissioner (SWM) moves to another department at the same time.

- **Week 1:** The new Commissioner orders a review of "all state-driven pilots". The IMC nodal officer's attendance at steering committee meetings drops.
- **Week 3:** The new Additional Commissioner prefers IMC's own SWM 2026 portal (F3): "one portal, ours". Transfer-station clerks stop sealing lots because the order that asked them to was an internal note signed by the old Additional Commissioner.
- **Week 5:** City-stream custody events fall by about 70% (**ASSUMPTION**). The pilot's volume gate is at risk.
- **What saves it, if anything:** (1) the joint order and a tripartite MoU that name **posts, not people**; (2) the vendor empanelment clause, which makes logging a *contract obligation* of the vendor rather than a favour from IMC staff; (3) EcoSure reading from IMC's own portal via an API, so there is nothing to "switch off"; (4) a 1-page handover brief and a 30-minute briefing for the new Commissioner in their first two weeks, led by UDHD, not by the operator.
- **What kills it:** If the MoU was never signed (the 4.1 outcome) and logging depends on staff goodwill, the programme is back to shops and drives only, at the ₹224/kg cost the PRD itself warns about (section 26.2).
- **Compounding risk:** If month 7 falls inside the 2027 municipal election code (F11), the new Commissioner cannot make new commitments even if willing. Section 23 handles the code only for incentives, not for new MoUs or renewed orders.

## 8. Fixes (in priority order)

1. **Add a "municipal stream" workflow (fixes G1, G8, G12).** New section 16.x: vehicle compartment → **first custody at the zonal transfer station or e-waste bay** (weighed, sealed, tagged with vehicle number and ward by a vendor or recycler clerk) → recycler gate (IMEI and serial scans, receiver weight) → attestation. No citizen code or incentive in this stream. Route evidence comes from IMC's existing SWM control-room GPS. Report KPIs **separately by stream** (city, agent or shop, drives, bulk, PRO) so city tonnes cannot hide household results.
2. **Replace the claimed baseline with a verified-baseline phase (fixes G4).** Manual pilot weeks 1–6 weigh the IMC flow and publish nothing; the verified figure becomes the baseline. Historic claims are never charted or compared in any report. Add the rule "claimed-versus-verified comparisons are steering-committee-confidential" to 24.1. Move the 8 t/month gate to *additional* tonnes above the verified baseline.
3. **Write a vendor transition clause and a recycler neutrality rule (fixes G5, G11).** Existing contracts run to term.
