## 2. Findings

### F1. Realistic yield: 100–300 kg per society drive, not tonnes

| Drive type | Evidence | Yield |
|---|---|---|
| Apartment complex (Bengaluru, Covid) | S1/S2: 1,533 kg / 30 complexes / 6,000 flats | **~51 kg per complex, ~0.26 kg per flat** |
| Residential complex (Rajasthan, with cash) | S15: 38.5 t / 150+ complexes | **≤ ~257 kg per complex** |
| Ward drop-point day (Pune) | S6: 280 kg / 71 donors / 30 centres | **~4 kg per donor, ~9 kg per centre** |
| Municipal mega-drive point (Hyderabad) | S9: 88 t / 531 points; S10: 15 t / 110 points | **~135–165 kg per point**; best points 450–510 kg |
| Volunteer door-to-door (Bengaluru) | S3: 421 kg in 2 hours | 421 kg per event |
| School (parents send with children) | S17 | **154 kg per school** |
| College (NSS/NCC) | S18 | **~1 t per campus** (includes large appliances) |
| Multi-institution college campaign | S16: 2 t / 50+ locations | ~40 kg per location |

A single 150–250 flat society will typically give **50–250 kg**, most of it by weight in 1–3 large appliances, with a long tail of cables, chargers and small items (S2: 42% "misc"). Participation is well below 100% (S1: 60% of complexes that signed up; per-flat participation not reported — **UNVERIFIED**, likely 10–30%).

**Implication for PRD:** "expected volume" entered by the secretary will be wildly optimistic. The drive needs a dispatch threshold and must be batched with other drives on one vehicle route, the same way `06` trips batch shops.

### F2. Drives pay for themselves only when batched and when large appliances are present

Saahas says e-waste revenue covered operations, but the small-item tail is negative value (S2); Indore vendors pay IMC up to ₹20/kg (S13). A 150 kg drive is ~₹1,500–3,000 of material value. A fixed per-category UPI incentive paid **per resident** (C6) plus a dedicated vehicle trip can exceed that. GHMC paid ₹20,874 for 510 kg at one point (~₹41/kg, S10) — funded by the municipality, not by material value.

### F3. The secretary is motivated by clean-up, recognition, and zero effort — not money

Evidence of what organisers value: "clean surroundings", awareness, involving children (S8, S17), federation-level campaigns (S1), civic recognition (GHMC/IMC mayor events, S10, S14). RWA secretaries already run WhatsApp groups; they want (a) one message they can forward, (b) a fixed date and truck that actually turns up, (c) a society-level certificate/summary for the AGM and social media, (d) no handling of money. **UNVERIFIED** (no survey found): many societies would prefer incentives pooled to the society fund or donated, rather than 80 separate ₹20–50 UPI payments.

C8 today gives the secretary a summary *after processing*, but no certificate, no drive page to forward, no reminders, and no option to pool incentives.

### F4. Per-resident registration, weighing and UPI is the throughput bottleneck

C8 says each resident "still gets their own receipt and incentive". With C1 (OTP sign-in), C3 (wipe confirmation per data-bearing item), C6 (UPI per pickup) and per-pickup weighing, a collector at a 60-resident drive does 60 sign-ins, 60 weighings, 60 payouts. Real drives run in 2–4 hour windows (S7: 9am–1pm; S3: 2 hours) with contactless drop at a common spot (S1). Residents without smartphones/UPI (elderly, domestic staff handing over for employers) are excluded. The C6 cap of 4 paid pickups per month is irrelevant here; the real abuse vector is non-residents registering against a drive to farm incentives.

### F5. Offices, colleges and large schools are often **bulk consumers**; handover must name the registered recycler

Rule 8 (W1): an entity that used ≥1,000 Schedule I EEE units in a financial year must hand e-waste only to a registered producer, refurbisher or recycler. Most IT offices, engineering colleges, hospitals and many large private schools cross 1,000 units (counting PCs, phones, lamps, fans, ACs — **UNVERIFIED** per institution). Most RWAs do not (households are consumers). C8 and P8 have no bulk-consumer flag and no receipt naming the recycler as legal receiver (wave-1 `01` G2 and `03` F2 already flag this as High).

### F6. Government offices cannot hand over to EcoSure shops at all

GFR 2017 Rule 217(v): e-waste lots must be sold to bidders with valid recycler/pre-processor registration on the auction and delivery dates (S19). Practice: forward auction on GeM or MSTC with registered-recycler-only eligibility (S20–S22). The Special Campaign (2–31 Oct yearly, preparatory phase from 15 Sep) generates large volumes — ₹370 cr scrap revenue in 2.0, ₹117 cr in two weeks of 3.0 (S23, S24) — but even a whole ministry's e-scrap can be small (DARPG ₹38,510, S25). EcoSure's role for government offices is therefore **not collection**, but: (a) helping the office prepare a GFR-10 lot list by category and weight, (b) listing only EcoSure-attested registered recyclers as eligible bidders, and (c) providing custody attestation after the recycler lifts the lot. Government schools fall under the same rules as their department (state GFR equivalents — **UNVERIFIED** for MP's financial code specifics).

### F7. The strongest drives are ULB-anchored, calendar-driven, and use institutions as collection points

GHMC (S9–S12), PMC/PEHEL (S7), IMC (S13, S14) and RSPCB/Godrej (S15) all show the same pattern: a municipal or SPCB anchor, a fixed city-wide date, many collection points (housing societies, schools, parks), announcements through existing garbage vehicles, and cash or rewards. Schools reach households through children (S17), colleges bring volunteers and logistics (S16, S18). Indore specifically has a municipal door-to-door e-waste stream already (2–2.5 t/day, S13) and uses Swachhata Hi Seva (17 Sep–2 Oct) as its campaign window (S14). EcoSure C8 is framed as a secretary-initiated, one-society event — the weakest variant.

### F8. C8 has no data model

`03-domain-model.md` has `PickupRequest.mode = society_drive` but no `Drive` entity (host org, date, window, location, registrations, threshold, vehicle, gate pass, aggregate weight, certificate). Without it, "one gate pass", "society summary" and batching cannot be implemented, and `10-workflows.md` has no drive workflow at all.

## 3. Is C8 designed right?

**Direction right, mechanics wrong.** Society drives are the correct density wedge (one vehicle stop, one gate, 50–250 kg vs ~2–5 kg per doorstep pickup). But C8 is written as a household pickup multiplied by N: per-resident OTP, weighing and UPI, with no threshold, no batching, no host types beyond "secretary", no legal handling for bulk consumers, and no data model. It also misses the channel that actually produces tonnes in India: ULB/SPCB-anchored drive days using societies, schools and colleges as collection points.

## 4. What would make it the main channel

1. **Drive as a first-class object** with host types (RWA, school, college, office, ULB ward, government office) and a public drive page/WhatsApp link the host forwards.
2. **Drive calendar anchored to ULB/SPCB campaigns** (Swachhata Hi Seva 17 Sep–2 Oct, Special Campaign Oct, World Environment Day 5 Jun, Diwali clean-up) — the operator creates corridor-wide drive days; hosts opt in as collection points.
3. **Dispatch threshold + route batching**: a drive is confirmed only when registrations/estimated kg pass a corridor threshold (e.g. 25 registrations or 100 kg); otherwise merged with nearby drives or converted to a drop-box.
4. **Fast lane at the gate**: batch weighing by resident token (QR/short code), wipe confirmation collected at registration, UPI paid in bulk after the drive closes; walk-up residents without registration allowed as "anonymous donation" (no incentive).
5. **Incentive choice**: pay to resident, pool to society/school fund, or donate — host chooses a default.
6. **Host certificate** (drive-level: total kg by category, recycler name + CPCB registration, attestation numbers) — the secretary's real reward.
7. **Legal routing**: bulk-consumer flag → recycler-as-receiver receipt; government office → GFR/GeM/MSTC assist mode, never shop collection.

## 6. Score

**5 / 10** for C8 as written.

Right strategic bet (drives are the density wedge, and the PRD names them as such in `01` and `00`), but the feature is a thin story with five bullets, no data model, no workflow, per-resident mechanics that will not scale at a gate, no dispatch threshold against realistic 50–250 kg yields, no legal routing for bulk consumers or government offices, and no link to the ULB/SPCB campaign calendar that produces most real-world tonnage in Hyderabad, Pune and Indore. With changes 1, 2, 3 and 5 it could reach 8/10 and plausibly become the largest kg channel in the pilot.
