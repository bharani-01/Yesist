## 2. Findings

### F1. Indore has real, authorised recycler capacity, but Pithampur's own recycler is compromised
- MPPCB lists 11 authorised recyclers statewide (S1). Indore has Unique Eco Recycle, Samyak Computer and Primero Waste Solution, plus Moonstar Enterprises, an authorised **dismantler** on Sanwer Road (S1, S2).
- Capacity per the CPCB register: Indore has about 10,800 TPA across 3 recyclers (UER 6,000; Primero Waste 3,300; Samyak 1,500). Statewide it is about 58,880 TPA, concentrated in Sehore/Bhopal (S4, S5 — aggregator, **UNVERIFIED**). Sehore and Bhopal are roughly 190 km away, which matches the pilot design's "recycler within 150–250 km" fallback.
- Actual throughput is much lower than permitted capacity. UER reported processing about 450 t in FY24 (S7), and MP processed only 5,915 t in FY24 and 13,014 t in FY25 statewide (S6). **Implication:** recyclers want feedstock, which supports the offtake-agreement thesis.
- The only listed recycler in Pithampur is Hazargo (Dhar district), which is linked to a hazardous-waste fire on 21-04-2026, an operations halt and NGT proceedings (S15; the link to Hazargo is **UNVERIFIED**). Do not select it as the corridor recycler until MPPCB confirms its status.

### F2. IMC already runs a municipal e-waste channel that is larger than EcoSure's pilot ambition, and the PRD does not mention it
- IMC says it collects about 2–2.5 t/day of e-waste through door-to-door garbage vehicles, out of an estimated 10–12 t/day generated, and hands it to two vendors (S8). That is roughly 700–900 t/year already in a municipal stream.
- IMC runs Swachhotsav e-waste drives with dedicated vehicles, drop boxes and plans for door-to-door e-waste collection involving RWAs, schools and colleges (S9). It also announced a **paid** app for doorstep pickup of e-waste and junk (S10), which directly overlaps EcoSure's citizen pickup.
- IMC segregates waste six ways, including e-waste, and reports 98% source segregation (S12, S13). It also claims to be the first ULB to earn EPR credit, for plastic, through a PPP material recovery facility (S13, date **UNVERIFIED**).
- The "payment up to ₹20/kg" reported in S8 is ambiguous: it may be IMC paying vendors or vendors paying IMC. **UNVERIFIED**. Either way, a rate card and a flow already exist, and citizen incentive design must not undercut or duplicate them.
- **Implication:** the PRD names SPCB + state IT as sponsor, and shops/hubs as the collection layer. In Indore the dominant formal-leaning collector is the ULB. Without IMC, EcoSure competes with the city's flagship Swachh brand. With IMC, it gains vehicles, ward reach, RWA relationships and political cover.

### F3. MP has no standalone e-waste policy. The state instruments are old department orders plus the national SPCB action plan
- MPPCB's e-waste page carries state orders on safe disposal of government e-waste (2010, 2014) and a 2015 circular to all departments. Its annual reports stop at 2018-19, and its inventory is an old executive summary (S3). No 2024–26 MP e-waste policy was found; absence is **UNVERIFIED**.
- Under the national action plan, SPCBs file quarterly progress reports and must run drives against informal e-waste activity while helping informal actors formalise (S22). **Implication:** EcoSure's SPCB view should produce the evidence MPPCB already owes CPCB each quarter, such as drives, formalised units and formal tonnes. That makes it useful to the Board, not just another dashboard.
- Government and PSU offices in Indore buy e-waste disposal and degaussing via tenders and GeM, for example MPSEDC and LIC Indore (S17). This bulk-consumer B2G volume is steady and high-grade, and state orders already require formal disposal. The PRD has no "government office / bulk consumer" segment.

### F4. Informal market: household e-waste is overwhelmingly informal, and Indore's scrap cluster is known but not studied
- Recyclers say only about 10% of formal MP volume comes from households; the rest is corporate, showrooms and government (S7). This matches the PRD's premise that "cash beats paperwork".
- Informal dismantling (wire burning, acid baths) is reported in slum and industrial areas of Bhopal and Indore (S21, low-tier academic, **UNVERIFIED** at street level). Scrap trading clusters around Siyaganj / New Siyaganj / Malgodam, Anoop Nagar and Khajrana (S21 directories). A Rewa field study shows tier-2 MP kabadis selling boards on to Delhi wholesale markets (S21), so high-value fractions may leave the state.
- IMC itself wants to "bridge scrap dealers and recycling firms" (S8). That is EcoSure's shop layer, which makes IMC a natural co-sponsor for shop onboarding.
- No primary ethnography of Indore's e-waste kabadi network exists. The pilot's "8 active shops" gate is therefore untested against real density.

### F5. Pithampur carries serious reputational risk, and its producer base is thinner than the PRD implies
- In Jan 2025, bringing Union Carbide waste to Ramky's Pithampur facility triggered mass protests, stone-pelting, self-immolation attempts and prohibitory orders, with opposition spreading to Indore (S14). A 2026 hazardous-waste fire in Sector 3 added to local anger (S15).
- Labelling a hazardous-waste custody programme "Indore + Pithampur" invites the question "is more toxic waste coming to Pithampur?" A hub or storage godown sited in Pithampur is the most likely trigger.
- Pithampur is dominated by auto, pharma and engineering units. EEE producers are few (Panasonic Energy, L&T ECC, Methodex, small units), and Indore's Electronic Complex at Pardeshipura holds the EEE MSMEs (S23). Many "Indore-class" mid-size producers may be registered with CPCB elsewhere and file EPR from HQ. The producer wedge (≥10 exporting producers) needs a verified local producer count from the CPCB EPR portal (**UNVERIFIED**).

### F6. MPSEDC is the obvious hosting and IT partner
- MPSEDC runs the State Data Centre (Bhopal), a DR centre (RailTel, ₹34.9 cr), a SOC/CSIRT link, e-tendering and a rate contract for MeitY-empanelled cloud via MSPs (S16). This fits the PRD's "state data centre or government-empanelled cloud" assumption exactly.
- MPSEDC also runs STP Indore and is itself an e-waste generator that tenders out disposal (S17), so it can act as both host and anchor bulk-consumer customer.

### F7. Is Indore the right pilot? Mostly yes, with conditions
| Factor | Evidence | Verdict |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Civic execution capacity | 8× cleanest city, 98% segregation, e-waste already segregated (S11–S13) | Strong |
| Recycler availability | 3 Indore recyclers + 1 dismantler; spare capacity (S1, S4–S7) | Strong |
| Political salience | CM-launched e-waste drive in Indore (S9) | Strong, but it belongs to IMC and the Urban Development department, not MPPCB |
| Informal competition | Dense scrap cluster; households ~10% formal (S7, S21) | A good test bed |
| Producer wedge | Pithampur not EEE-heavy (S23) | Weak / unverified |
| Pithampur branding | Carbide + 2026 fire (S14, S15) | Negative |
| Risk of "already solved" | IMC channel + paid app (S8, S10) | Needs a partnership rather than competition |

Indore also enables the Swachh Survekshan "Swachh City Partnership" mentoring model (S11): a proven Indore playbook can be passed to mentee cities, which is a better path to a second corridor than a cold start.

## 6. Score

**6 / 10** for Madhya Pradesh fit of PRD v2 as written.

- Indore as the pilot city: **7.5 / 10**. It has the strongest civic execution in India, authorised recyclers with spare capacity, visible political interest in e-waste, and a dense informal market to compete against.
- Deductions: the PRD ignores IMC's existing e-waste channel and paid pickup app (the biggest local fact), bundles Pithampur despite live hazardous-waste protest history and a recycler under suspension, overstates the local EEE producer base, and leaves out MPSEDC and government bulk consumers.
- These fixes are cheap: mostly sponsor and stakeholder edits plus Week 0 verification tasks.
