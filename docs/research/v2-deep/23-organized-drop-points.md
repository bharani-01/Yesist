## 2. Findings

### F1. Organised drop points already exist at scale in India, but almost all are already tied to a PRO or brand recycler
- Samsung runs bins at service centres (1,200+ centres engaged) via PROs Kar Parivartan, RLG, Rapidue (B1–B3). Croma has had a bin in every store since 2016 (JustDispose, R Planet) (B4, B5). Reliance Digital routes through The Pegasus (B6). Karo Sambhav claims 2,000+ retail partners and is actively hiring to sign more retailers, OEM stores and service networks (B9–B11).
- **Implication:** Chain retailers and brand service centres in Indore are very likely already contractually committed to a PRO. They will join EcoSure only if (a) their PRO/recycler is on EcoSure, or (b) EcoSure is a visibility/receipt layer that does not divert material. Asking them to switch recycler is a non-starter. Whether Indore stores of these chains actually operate bins today is **UNVERIFIED** (no store-level Indore list checked).

### F2. Bins alone collect very little; the evidence says kabadi doorstep supply dominates
- Only ~0.5% of households use workplace/colony bins versus ~45% kabadiwalas and ~7% formal centres (M1). ICEA/Accenture calls drop-off boxes and drives "not institutional" and low-volume (M2). IIMA found collection "not convenient" and 5–15% formal capture (M5). Samsung explicitly pays no incentive (B1); IIMA found only one brand offering money (M6).
- **Implication:** Drop points are a **complement**, strongest for small, portable, data-bearing items (phones, chargers, laptops, batteries) that households hoard (M3: 10–15% stored at home). Large appliances and bulk volume will still come through doorstep kabadi / micro-tier collectors. EcoSure's UPI incentive (C6) is the differentiator that brand bins lack — but only if it works for walk-in drop-off, which v2 does not specify (see F5).

### F3. Value to each organised actor is different; producer payments are indirect
| Actor | Likely value | Likely objection |
|-------|--------------|------------------|
| Mobile repair shop (independent) | Weekly cash for its **own repair scrap** (dead screens, swollen batteries, boards); footfall from citizens coming with old phones who may buy repair/accessories; a "government-listed" signboard | Already sells parts/boards to informal dismantlers at better price; resale of repairable phones is its margin — which is unregistered refurbishment (rule 4, wave-1 G7) |
| Electronics retailer (chain) | CSR/ESG and awareness reporting; footfall; link to exchange offers | Already tied to a PRO (F1); store staff time; floor space; will not accept liability for data or batteries |
| Electronics retailer (independent / multi-brand dealer) | Footfall and upgrade sales; handling fee; exchange-offer co-marketing | No existing PRO tie-up → **best new supply**, but needs a principal recycler |
| Brand service centre (franchise) | Brand compliance KPI; awareness reporting; audit trail for brand take-back | Brand PRO controls routing; EcoSure is useful only as the recycler-side custody chain |
| Producer | Traceable brand take-back reporting; awareness (rule 6(3)) | No statutory EPR credit from drop-point weight (wave-1 `01` §2.6, `17` F1) |

- "Producer payments" to drop points exist only as voluntary PRO/brand handling fees or through certificate value passed down by the recycler (wave-1 `17`). EcoSure should not promise a producer payment stream.
- Trade-in is structurally separate: Reliance says recycled products are "not eligible for exchange offers" (B6); PROs are chasing exchange/buyback streams directly (B10). EcoSure should interoperate (receipt that an exchanged device entered the chain) rather than run trade-in.

### F4. Rule 13(1) gives dealers a legitimate but subordinate role
- 2022 rules removed dealer duties that existed in 2016 (R3); rule 13(1) keeps dealers only as optional helpers of a producer, EPR staying entirely with the producer (R1). Only registered producers/recyclers/refurbishers may "collect" (R2).
- So a retailer or repair shop is lawful as a **documented drop point of a named registered recycler or producer/PRO** — the same principal requirement wave-1 `01` proposes for shops. Drop points must not dismantle, must be cleared periodically, and must not stockpile loose batteries (R4).
- Large retail chains using ≥1,000 EEE units a year may themselves be bulk consumers (rule 8) for their own IT waste; that is separate from customer drop-off. **UNVERIFIED** how MPPCB treats customer drop-off bins in stores.

### F5. PRD v2 does not model organised drop points
- `04-consumer.md` C7 lists "approved and provisional shops" as drop-off points, and the Summary promises "drop-off at a shop". But C5/C6 receipts and incentive are defined around **pickups** ("when the pickup is marked collected"); there is no walk-in counter flow, no counter receipt, no bin, no clearance cycle.
- `05-local-recycle-shop.md` treats every shop as a **buying collector** (pickup queue, rate card, weekly settlement, advances). A Croma store or Samsung service centre does not buy material or run pickups; it needs a lighter "hold and hand over" role with a handling fee or no payment.
- No field records an existing PRO/brand tie-up, so material already committed to a brand PRO could be **double reported** (EcoSure attestation plus PRO's own records) — a trust risk for producer exports (`08` P3).
- `08-manufacturer.md` P7 (take-back programme) funds citizen incentives but cannot designate a brand's own service centres as EcoSure drop points or see drop-point volumes.
- Repair-shop **own scrap** (a steady, high-value, battery-heavy stream) has no lot type; the refurbishment prohibition proposed in wave-1 is not yet in the PRD.

### F6. Comparison with micro / kabadi supply
| Dimension | Organised drop points | Micro-tier / kabadi collectors |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------------------------|
| Volume evidence | Low (0.5% bin use, M1; M2) | Dominant (45%, M1; 60–90%, M2/M3) |
| Item mix | Small, portable, data-bearing, batteries | Large appliances, mixed household scrap |
| Data-wipe trust | High at brand service centres | Low; needs collector-assisted wipe (C3) |
| Identity / KYC | Easy (GSTIN, fixed premises) | Hard (no premises, shared phones; wave-1 `14`) |
| Fraud risk | Low weight fraud; risk of double-reporting PRO material | Weight and ghost-pickup fraud; provisional cap needed |
| Leakage risk | Repair shops cannibalise parts / refurbish | Stripping boards, compressors, copper before handover |
| Political risk (Indore) | Low | High if enrolled shops displace kabadis (wave-1 `14` F-Indore) |
| Onboarding cost to EcoSure | Low per point, but needs chain/brand deals | Higher per collector; field enrolment |

Net: organised points add **legitimacy, visibility and data-safe capture of small devices** at low onboarding cost; kabadi/micro collectors carry the **volume**. EcoSure needs both, and the pilot should measure yield per channel rather than assume.

### F7. Indore specifics
- IMC already runs e-waste drop boxes during Swachhata campaigns and a paid scrap-pickup app (I1–I3). This is both a competitor and a partner: IMC offices/MRFs as public drop points (wave-1 `01` notes SWM Rules 2026 MRF deposition centres).
- No Karo Sambhav collection centre in Indore appeared in its listing (B8, **UNVERIFIED**), so independent multi-brand dealers and repair clusters in Indore may be uncontracted — the most promising new drop-point supply. Store-level presence of Croma/Reliance/Samsung bins in Indore is **UNVERIFIED**.

## 4. Recommended PRD changes (file + change)

Proposed edits for the PRD owner; not applied.

1. **`02-roles-rbac.md` and `05-local-recycle-shop.md` — add a `drop_point` operating mode for `local_shop` organisations.**
   > "Organisation attribute `collection_mode`: `buying_collector` (current S1–S10) or `drop_point`. A drop point accepts walk-in handovers only, does not buy material or run pickups, holds sealed bins, and hands over to the hub or its principal on a clearance schedule. Drop points never dismantle, never test-and-resell, and hold loose batteries only in a sealed battery container below the corridor cap. Sub-types: `repair_shop`, `electronics_retailer`, `brand_service_centre`, `public_office` (IMC/MRF)."
   Drop-point home screen leads with bin fill level, next clearance date and handling fee due (if any), not rate card or advances.

2. **`04-consumer.md` C5/C6/C7 — define the walk-in drop-off flow.**
   > "C7a Walk-in drop-off (Phase 1): citizen shows a WhatsApp/QR handover code or gives a phone number; counter staff record categories, counts and approximate weight, confirm data wipe (C3 applies), and seal the item in the bin. Citizen receives a WhatsApp receipt immediately. The UPI incentive is paid when the drop point records the handover for `buying_collector` shops, and when the bin is weighed at hub receipt for `drop_point` mode (receipt says 'incentive pending weighing'). C7 list filters by category accepted, 'battery accepted', and 'wipe help available'."

3. **`05-local-recycle-shop.md` S1 — declare existing take-back tie-ups and prevent double reporting.**
   > "Onboarding asks whether the organisation already hands e-waste to a PRO, brand programme or recycler, and records the partner name and CPCB registration. Material in an existing brand/PRO channel is either routed through that partner's recycler if on EcoSure, or excluded from EcoSure attestation. Lots carry `channel_owner` (EcoSure / named PRO / named brand)."
   Mirror `channel_owner` and `collection_mode` in `03-domain-model.md` (`Organization`, `Lot`).

4. **`05-local-recycle-shop.md` — new S11 Repair-shop scrap and reuse boundary (Phase 1).**
   > "Repair shops can file their own repair scrap (screens, batteries, boards, dead handsets) as lot type `generated_scrap`, weighed and photographed, with batteries in a separate battery stream. Collected citizen devices must not be repaired or resold (refurbisher activity, rule 4/7). Working devices may be flagged for routing to a registered refurbisher (Phase 2)."

5. **`08-manufacturer.md` P7 — add P9 Brand drop-point designation (Phase 2).**
   > "A producer or its PRO can designate its authorised service centres and partner retailers in a corridor as EcoSure drop points (after operator approval and principal-recycler link). The producer sees drop-point counts, volume by category, clearance timeliness and attestation links. Reports state: 'Take-back reporting only. Does not create or offset EPR obligations; EPR is met only through CPCB certificates (rule 13).' Producer-funded handling fees or incentive top-ups flow through the recycler/PRO escrow, never a state account."

6. **`00-overview.md` metrics and `13-roadmap.md` pilot gates — measure channel yield.**
   > "Report kg/month and cost per kg by channel: doorstep pickup, micro/kabadi collector, buying shop, drop point (by sub-type), society drive. Pilot target: ≥ 15 organised drop points in Indore (mix of repair shops, independent dealers, one brand service network, IMC offices). Review at week 12; drop points below a sponsor-set yield are converted to awareness-only listings."

7. **`14-open-questions.md` — add:**
   > "OQ-xx: Will Croma/Reliance/Samsung/other brand PROs in Indore allow their store bins to be listed on EcoSure, and on what data-sharing terms?"
   > "OQ-xx: MPPCB position on customer drop-off bins in retail stores and repair shops as collection points of a recycler (clearance frequency, battery quantity limit)."
   > "OQ-xx: Handling fee for drop points — none, flat per clearance, or per kg — and who pays (recycler, producer, scheme)."

## 5. Score

**4 / 10** for how well PRD v2 models organised collection points.

- **Earns the 4:** v2 names drop-off as a citizen channel (C7), has a data-wipe gate that suits phones, a UPI incentive that brand bins lack, and a producer take-back pool that could fund drop-point campaigns. Wave-1 legal fixes (principal recycler, refurbishment ban) would also cover drop points.
- **Holds it back:** no drop-point role or walk-in flow; every shop is modelled as a buying collector; no handling of existing PRO/brand tie-ups (double-reporting risk); no repair-shop scrap stream; producers cannot designate service centres; no channel-yield measurement even though the evidence says bins collect little.
- **Path to 7:** changes 1–3 and 6 (all PRD/domain-model work), plus answers to the OQs above from two or three Indore retailers/brand PROs before build.
