# 29 — Fraud Red Team: Money and Paper Flows in EcoSure PRD v2

**Agent:** 29 of 36 (v2 deep-research swarm)
**Date:** 2026-09-27
**PRD files reviewed:** `04-consumer.md`, `05-local-recycle-shop.md`, `10-workflows.md`, `12-nfr-security.md`. **Angle:** Attack every flow where money or evidence moves: the citizen UPI incentive, shop settlements, advances, dual weighing, photos, the offline queue, OTP identity, operator powers, and custody attestations. For each scheme, rate likelihood and impact, check whether v2 has a control, and state the control it needs.
**Status:** Research only. No PRD files were edited.

**Bottom line:** v2 gets the architecture right. It has idempotent payouts, dual weighing at every hand-off, recycler-issued attestations, and an append-only audit log. But almost every money trigger rests on evidence created by the party that gets paid. The citizen incentive fires on the shop's own `collected` record, with no confirmation from the citizen. The shop's settlement and advance cap both rest on the hub's receipt weight, and the float that pays them is scheme money, so the hub isn't risking its own. Photos are sealed bags, which all look alike. Offline capture time is whatever the device clock says. The operator sets rates, approves shops, resolves disputes, reverses incentives, and manages the float, with no maker-checker. The only incentive-specific control is "4 paid pickups per citizen per month". A citizen is a phone number, so that cap costs a SIM card to get around. **Score: 4.5 / 10.**

## 1. Money map (what triggers payment, and who holds the evidence)

| Money flow | Trigger in v2 | Evidence created by | Funded by | Who loses on fraud |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|-----------|--------------------|
| Citizen UPI incentive | Pickup → `collected` (`04` C6, `10` §1) | **Shop** (weigh record, bag photo, wipe confirmation; can be offline) | Scheme budget / producer pool (SP-04) | Scheme. The reversal in C6 can't practically claw money back from a citizen's UPI account |
| Shop settlement | Hub receipt weight, paid ≤7 days (`05` S5, `10` §7) | **Hub** (receiver weigh record) | Settlement float run by the contracted operator (`00` §Operations) | Scheme float, then the hub when the recycler rejects or downgrades the lot later |
| Shop advance | 40% of average weekly received value over 4 weeks (`05` S5) | Hub receipts (the history an attacker can pump) | Float | Scheme float (default on exit) |
| Undisputed portion during a dispute | Lower of the two readings is paid on schedule (`10` §4.5) | Shop and hub | Float | Either side, depending on who under- or over-reads |
| Producer evidence / attestation | Recycler issues after processing (`10` §6) | **Recycler** | n/a (paper value to producers) | Producers and SPCB credibility |

Structural observation: the recycler is the only party in the chain whose evidence is independent of the party being paid, and it comes last. It arrives weeks after the citizen incentive and the shop settlement have already been paid. Under v2, fast money means the money is gone before anything is verified. That's acceptable only if someone upstream (the shop) underwrites the loss and can be charged back.

## 3. Findings

### F1. The citizen incentive rests on the shop's word alone
The C6 trigger is "marked collected", and only the shop marks it. That can happen offline. No citizen action is needed at the moment of payment. Every high-likelihood scheme (T1, T2, T3, T12) exploits this. The single cheapest, strongest control is a citizen handover code, verified offline, required for the incentive. It turns the payment from something the shop asserts into something both parties confirm. The phrase "paid on `collected`, not later" (`10` §1) can stay, as long as `collected` requires the code.

### F2. "Per citizen" really means "per SIM", so the 4/month cap is almost cosmetic
`users.phone` is the only unique identity (`03` §constraints). Prepaid SIMs and virtual WhatsApp numbers are cheap, so the cap stops only honest users. The caps that matter are per payee bank account (from VPA validation), per normalised address, and per device, each with a monthly rupee ceiling. The configured cap also needs a **per-shop** incentive budget, because the shop is where collusion concentrates.

### F3. Money leaves before independent evidence arrives, and nobody underwrites the gap
The incentive is paid at collection, the shop within 7 days of hub receipt, and the advance on hub-received value. The recycler's weight and grade arrive weeks later. When the recycler rejects or down-grades a lot (sand, water, non-e-waste), v2 records a return-freight settlement line (`10` §5.4) but doesn't push the loss back to the originating shop. The incentive reversal in C6 has no recovery path. The fix is chargeback liability that follows the witness: shops underwrite incentives they witnessed and material they sent, and hubs underwrite receipt weights they recorded.

### F4. Photos and weighing prove little in the form v2 specifies
Sealed bags are interchangeable. SHA-256 dedup only catches lazy fraud. Padding at source passes dual weighing because both scales weigh the same padded bag. What turns photos into evidence is **unique numbered seal tags**, camera-only capture with signed GPS and time, perceptual-hash dedup, category weight bands, and calibrated scales on record. Device count and IMEI hash for data-bearing items catch round-tripping and resale, which weight alone can't see (`16` F2).

### F5. Advances and micro-tier onboarding together form a bust-out path
Provisional micro shops run before review. Advance caps are based on four weeks of hub-received value, which can be pumped, and recovery happens only from future settlements. Nothing covers exit, dedup across the owner's relatives, or who absorbs default. OQ-71 lowers the percentage, which doesn't fix the design. The advance basis should be recycler-accepted value, with rupee ceilings per tier, no advances while provisional, and a funded default-loss line in the float.

### F6. The operator concentrates every money lever with no maker-checker
Rate cards, approvals, disputes, reversals, float, and support-channel changes all sit with one contracted operator. The audit log records wrongdoing but doesn't prevent it. Government programmes need two-person control on money-changing actions and an independent reconciliation owned by the department. The operator is a data processor under contract (`12` §5); it shouldn't also be the only financial controller.

### F7. `12` §7 "anomaly detection" is a placeholder
There are no rules, thresholds, flag types, owners, SLAs, or consequences. Without them it won't be built or won't be acted on, which is the same "flagged, never checked" failure `18` F6 documents at SPCBs. The PRD needs a named rule set (see Section 4, change P7), each rule with an owner, an SLA, and an automatic consequence (hold incentive, hold settlement line, freeze advance).

### F8. v2's structural advantage is real, and worth protecting
Citizen-origin custody with counterparty weights and recycler-issued attestations is much harder to fake than CPCB's recycler self-declaration model (`18` P3/P4). Most fixes here are cheap additions to existing records: codes, seal numbers, counts, chargeback lines, maker-checker. None needs a new subsystem.

## 5. Score

**4.5 / 10** for v2's resistance to money and paper fraud.

**In v2's favour:** idempotent payouts with daily reconciliation; dual weighing at every hand-off; a provisional cap; an append-only audit of money actions; recycler-issued attestations with verify-by-number; and "undisputed weight paid" that keeps honest shops liquid.

**Against v2:** the shop alone decides whether the incentive is paid; the identity cap works per SIM; there's no chargeback, so every loss lands on the scheme; photos and weights don't prove origin, contents, or units; the advance basis can be pumped, with no default owner; the operator holds every money lever without maker-checker; and anomaly detection is a placeholder.

With P1–P4, the score rises to about 7. Adding P5–P8 gets it to about 8, and P9–P11 to about 8.5. The remaining risk is collusion between a real citizen and a shop over real material. No system can fully stop that, so it has to be priced out through differentiated incentives (`16` R5) and per-shop budgets.
