## 2. Findings

### F1. NAMASTE already covers kabadi collectors and gives EcoSure a ready-made identity and benefits rail
- Waste pickers became the third NAMASTE component from FY 2024-25 (SFC approval 20.06.2024) [S1, S3].
- Target group explicitly includes **itinerant waste buyers**, sorters and workers at "repair facilities", with a minimum 6 months in the occupation [S2].
- Occupation can be proven by **"recent receipts from sale of scrap, endorsement by scrap shop owners / other registered Waste Pickers"** or membership of a recognised waste-picker organisation [S2]. EcoSure weigh slips and settlement statements could serve as this evidence.
- After ULB validation and e-KYC, the NAMASTE MIS generates a unique ID and an **occupational photo ID card "authorizing them for Wastepicking related activities in the concerned ULB/RLB"** [S1, S2].
- Benefits: Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY (₹5 lakh/family/year, de-duplicated), PPE kits, OSH and skill training, pre-matric scholarship for children, facilitated e-Shram registration, capital subsidy for collection vehicles for SHGs (up to 50% on ₹5 lakh), and support to collectives running 750 Dry Waste Collection Centres [S1, S2, S5].
- Profiling agencies (CSOs/CSCs/ROs/ULBs) are paid ₹250 per validated worker [S2].
- Scale: 3,78,547 profiled and 2,52,163 e-KYC validated per PIB [S4]; the Hindu cites 1.3 lakh profiled in the Ministry's 2025-26 annual report [S6]. Figures differ by date.
- **Risk:** the waste-picker component was sanctioned only for 2024-25 to 2025-26, "may be continued" after evaluation [S1]. An expansion to 2030-31 at ₹498.73 crore has been proposed but approval is **UNVERIFIED** [S6]. EcoSure must not make NAMASTE linkage a hard dependency.

### F2. SWM Rules 2026 (in force 1 Apr 2026) replace the 2016 Rules and now require ULB registration of waste pickers on a central portal
- SWM Rules 2016 Rule 15(c)/(d) required ULBs to recognise waste-picker organisations, issue ID cards and integrate them into door-to-door collection [S7]. PRD research and prompts that cite "SWM Rules 2016" are now citing a **repealed** instrument [S8, S10].
- SWM Rules 2026 define both "waste picker" and "informal waste collector" (the latter explicitly includes "waste traders ... involved in ... sale and purchase of waste materials") [S8].
- ULBs must prepare a **ward-wise database of all personnel including informal waste pickers/collectors** and upload it to the CPCB centralised portal; register waste pickers annually **by 30 June**; and integrate them "reflecting it on the centralised portal" [S8, S9].
- State policy must cover integration of waste pickers "through identification, enumeration and registration with local body or third party agency authorised by local body" within one year of notification, and contracts must include "integration of informal sector" [S8].
- Generators (gated communities, RWAs >5,000 m², institutions) must hand recyclables to **authorised** waste pickers or recyclers [S8]. This creates a direct incentive for kabadis to seek authorisation — and a risk that non-authorised ones lose access to societies.
- Analysts note the 2026 Rules still lack concrete labour/social-security linkage and warn of displacement as segregation tightens [S10].
- Note: SWM Rules govern solid waste; e-waste is "special care waste" and remains governed by E-Waste Rules 2022. The informal-integration duty is therefore a **ULB** duty EcoSure can help discharge, not a duty on EcoSure.

### F3. E-Waste Rules 2022 give informal collectors no statutory status — only a receipt-based entry path into formal recycling
- Only manufacturers, producers, refurbishers and recyclers register on the CPCB portal; dismantlers and collectors do not [S12, S13].
- Registered entities "shall not deal with any unregistered manufacturer, producer, recycler and refurbisher" — but collectors are not in that list, so buying from them is permitted [S12].
- A recycler buying from the informal sector must upload "any sales receipt having name and address of the seller" [S13]. EcoSure's shop identity + weigh slip + lot record is exactly this evidence, which is the core value to kabadis and recyclers alike.
- ULBs must ensure e-waste found in MSW is channelised to registered recyclers [S12].
- **Implication:** a micro-tier shop that only **collects and aggregates** intact equipment is within the law. A shop that **dismantles, burns, acid-leaches or strips boards** is doing processing that needs SPCB consent/authorisation. The PRD does not draw this line.

### F4. Informal e-waste work is hazardous; ILO guidance is "formalise without destroying livelihoods"
- ILO estimates 60–85% of India's e-waste is handled informally; outright bans "could cause a large number of workers to lose their livelihoods"; recommends transition under ILO Recommendation 204, OSH, social protection, cooperatives and the low-cost **WARM** safety training piloted in New Delhi [S21, S22, S23].
- Seelampur KAP study (n=220): only 12% used PPE, 24% knew what e-waste is, median hair lead 8 mg/kg [S24].
- Moradabad: formalisation push "has not benefited either the industry or its workers"; police control and rent-seeking; hazardous conditions and employment insecurity [S25]; local health surveys attribute ~80% of ailments to respiratory causes (single academic quoted; **UNVERIFIED**) [S26].
- Children: the Child and Adolescent Labour Act Schedule lists "work involving exposure to ... electronic waste" (Part A item 34 — adolescents prohibited to work, children prohibited to help) and "rag picking and scavenging" (Part B item 44 — children prohibited even in family enterprises) [S20]. Family-run kabadi units are a known child-labour setting.

### F5. Formalisation has a documented backlash pattern — including in Indore, the v2 pilot city
- Seelampur: annual MCD raids and sealing; police extortion of ₹50,000–1 lakh per truck; distrust of any "compliance programme" given past harassment [S28, S29, S30].
- Seelampur incomes reportedly fell from ₹700 to ₹300/day as work moves to formal plants; women especially cannot follow work to distant factories [S27].
- **Indore:** IMC issued ID cards (250 in 2016, ~2,000 by 2018) with only **one-year validity**, then — per the Alliance of Indian Waste-pickers — evicted and penalised pickers before Swachh Survekshan 2019; pickers forced to sell to MRF operators below market [S31, S32]. The city's own Smart City page concedes "with IMC taking over the waste collection, rag pickers were rendered jobless" [S33]. Over 3,000 pickers; 93% women [S31].
- **Implication for SP-10 (Indore + Pithampur):** EcoSure will be read locally against this history. A government platform that pays citizens UPI incentives to route e-waste to *enrolled* shops, while non-enrolled kabadis lose supply, repeats the Indore MRF pattern unless enrolment is open, fair and protective.

### F6. e-Shram and Aadhaar constrain how onboarding can be designed
- e-Shram: free, Aadhaar + Aadhaar-linked mobile + bank account; age 16+; not EPFO/ESIC/income-tax payer; gives UAN and PMSBY accident cover; 31.89 crore registrations as of Aug 2026 [S14, S15]. NCO 9611 "Garbage and Recycling Collectors" is the likely occupation code [S16, **UNVERIFIED** against MoLE master].
- Aadhaar Act s.7: a State may require Aadhaar for a Consolidated-Fund-funded benefit only via a **s.7 gazette notification** and must offer **"alternate and viable means of identification"**; s.8 requires consent and disclosure of alternatives [S17, S18]. PRD S1 makes "Aadhaar-verified owner phone" mandatory with no alternate — non-compliant if shop float/advances are scheme-funded (SP-04).
- Many informal workers — particularly women and migrants — lack an Aadhaar-linked phone in their own name; mandatory Aadhaar-phone binding excludes them (known-market, consistent with NAMASTE's need for resource organisations to assist e-KYC [S1]).
- DPDP Rules 2025 allow State processing for subsidies/benefits without consent under notified standards, but require intimation, purpose limitation and security [S19]. Worker data must not be repurposed for enforcement without a legal basis.

### F7. Proven integration models run through collectives and interface organisations, not individual app sign-up
- GIZ/adelphi: partner via NGOs, cooperatives and trusted local "nodal" leaders; payments must be "realistic ... to bridge the price gap"; offer IDs, PPE, training, social benefits, advocacy [S34].
- Karo Sambhav: vans collect in bulk so aggregators need not dismantle; help opening bank accounts and registering businesses; on-time automated payments [S35].
- Chintan trains kabadiwalas/itinerant buyers on e-waste hazards and rules [S36]; SWaCH (Pune) cooperative model is cited in the SWM Rules 2026 explainer [S8 companion doc].
- NAMASTE itself funds Resource Organisations (₹10,000/month per DWCC) to handhold collectives [S1].

## 4. Gaps

1. **No itinerant/no-premises path.** Collector identity is tied to a shop.
2. **No alternate to Aadhaar**, and no s.7 notification step in the sponsor checklist.
3. **Scope of permitted activity undefined** for micro shops (collect/aggregate vs dismantle/process).
4. **No social-protection linkage** (NAMASTE ID, e-Shram UAN, PM-JAY, PPE) even though government rails exist and would make EcoSure membership valuable beyond price.
5. **No worker-data purpose limitation** for SPCB/ULB users; risk of the platform being perceived as an enforcement dragnet.
6. **No child-labour safeguard** despite e-waste being a Schedule Part A hazardous process.
7. **No backlash monitoring**: no metric for non-enrolled kabadi displacement, no grievance channel, no worker-organisation seat in governance.
8. **Regulatory reference is stale**: SWM Rules 2016 are repealed by SWM Rules 2026 (effective 1 Apr 2026).
9. **No collective/SHG onboarding**: NAMASTE and GIZ evidence favour collectives; PRD only has individual owners.
10. **Scheme-continuity risk**: NAMASTE waste-picker component sanction ended FY 2025-26; continuation **UNVERIFIED**.

## 6. Score

**5 / 10** for informal-sector integration and protection in PRD v2.

Rationale: v2 fixes the economic reasons informal collectors avoid formal channels (weekly pay, advances, no minimum, hub freight, Hindi/offline, no GSTIN). But it treats informal workers purely as shop supply, not as workers the state has statutory and scheme obligations toward. It ignores NAMASTE, e-Shram and the SWM Rules 2026 ULB registry, excludes itinerant collectors through shop-shaped KYC, uses Aadhaar without the legally required alternate, sets no limits on hazardous processing or child labour, and has no safeguard against the Indore-style displacement backlash in its own pilot city. R1–R6 would take this to roughly 8/10 at low build cost, since most are policy, onboarding fields and manual Phase 1 operations.
